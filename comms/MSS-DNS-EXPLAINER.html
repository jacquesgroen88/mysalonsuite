My Salon Suite: Email and Booking Domain Setup

What we are asking IT to add, why it is set up this way, and exactly what changes for the people receiving the emails.

The short version

The leasing system sends email on your behalf: tour confirmations, reminders, nurture sequences and member onboarding. For those emails to land in inboxes rather than spam, the receiving mail providers need proof that the system is authorised to send as My Salon Suite.

That proof is a small set of DNS records. We are putting them on a subdomain of mysalonsuite.com rather than on the main domain itself. The rest of this sheet explains why that is the right call, and it is not only convention: your main domain is currently one step away from a technical limit that adding us to it would break.

Why a subdomain instead of mysalonsuite.com

1. Your main domain is at 9 of its 10 permitted lookups

SPF is the record that lists who is allowed to send email as your domain. The standard caps it at 10 DNS lookups. Go past that and the record is treated as broken, and mail providers can begin failing or distrusting mail from the domain.

The record on mysalonsuite.com today authorises three senders:

  • Google Workspace, for staff mail
  • Salesforce and Pardot
  • Mailchimp

Between them they already consume 9 of the 10 available lookups. Adding this platform to that same record needs four more, taking it to 13. That would push the record past the limit, and the consequence would not be limited to the leasing emails: staff mail, Salesforce and Mailchimp all authenticate through that one record.

Putting the leasing system on its own subdomain gives it a separate SPF record with its own fresh budget, and leaves your existing one untouched.

2. Sending reputation stays separated

A newly activated automated sender starts with no reputation at the mailbox providers and has to earn one. If it sends from the main domain, every bounce or spam complaint from a leasing sequence attaches to the same domain reputation as your invoices, staff email and Salesforce campaigns.

On its own subdomain that reputation is built and contained separately. If a sequence ever performs badly, corporate mail is unaffected.

3. It is additive and fully reversible

Every record is scoped to the subdomain only. Specifically, this setup does not:

  • change or replace your existing MX records, so Google Workspace mail routing is untouched
  • edit your existing SPF record
  • change your DMARC policy
  • affect any existing address at @mysalonsuite.com

The MX records in the list below apply only to the subdomain, and exist so that bounce and reply handling works for the sending service. Deleting the subdomain records at any point returns everything to exactly its current state.

Does it still look like My Salon Suite to the recipient? Yes. The sender name shown in the inbox is My Salon Suite Blaine, and the address underneath sits on your own domain rather than a third party one. Because the subdomain belongs to mysalonsuite.com, it also aligns correctly under your existing DMARC record, so it will report cleanly alongside your other senders.

Replies are handled separately from all of this. A reply goes to whichever monitored mailbox you nominate, normally the site leader's own @mysalonsuite.com address, so nobody has to watch a new inbox.

The subdomain label, and why this one. The label is mn, giving mn.mysalonsuite.com.

It is deliberately scoped to Minnesota. mysalonsuite.com is the national domain, so a generic label such as mail would read as one region claiming the brand's mail subdomain, and that is a fair thing for corporate to question. A regional label asks for nothing beyond Minnesota's own operations, touches no record any other region or the corporate marketing stack relies on, and gives corporate a pattern any other region could reuse rather than a one-off exception.

The mechanism itself is already in use on this domain: go.mysalonsuite.com points at Act-On on its own subdomain, for exactly the same deliverability reason.

The friendly half of the address is set inside the platform and needs no DNS work, so it can change at any time. Our proposal is that emails show as My Salon Suite Blaine from stacey@mn.mysalonsuite.com, with replies going to Stacey's existing @mysalonsuite.com mailbox so there is no new inbox for anyone to watch.
